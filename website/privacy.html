Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 22, 2026

1. Summary

Veto is an app blocker for iPhone. This policy is short because the short version is the whole version: Veto does not collect, transmit, sell or share your personal data. There is nothing to collect. The app has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking. Everything you create with Veto — your rules, your settings, your statistics — is stored only on your device and never leaves it.

2. What Veto is

Veto ("Veto", "the app", "we", "us") lets you create schedules that restrict which apps can be opened and when — for example, blocking social media every evening. It is built on Apple's Screen Time technologies (Family Controls, Managed Settings and Device Activity) and runs entirely on your iPhone. It requires iOS 16.4 or later.

3. Data stored on your device

Veto keeps the following information in local on-device storage, shared between the Veto app and its system extensions (such as the shield and widget extensions) through a private app-group container:

  • Blocking rules — the schedules you create: which apps are restricted, at what times, on which days, and how strict each rule is.
  • Settings — your preferences, such as theme, grace limits, and notification toggles.
  • Statistics — a local log of blocking events (shield appearances, grace bypasses, protected time) used to show you your own history on the Stats screen.
  • Widget and shield configuration — appearance settings for the home-screen widget and the blocking shield.

This information exists only on your device. We have no servers that store it, no backups of it, and no way to read it.

4. Screen Time permissions

To block apps, Veto asks you to authorize Family Controls in Settings. This permission is used for one purpose only: enforcing the blocking schedules you create. The list of apps you choose to restrict and all schedule enforcement happen on your device through Apple's Screen Time framework. Revoking the permission in iOS Settings stops blocking but does not delete your rules.

5. Network activity

Veto makes exactly one kind of network request: a lookup to Apple's public iTunes Search API to fetch metadata (such as category names) for a fixed, built-in list of well-known apps used by the app picker. The request contains only public application identifiers — never your rules, settings, statistics, device identifiers, or anything about you. Apple receives this request and handles it under Apple's privacy policy.

Other than that, Veto connects to nothing: no analytics services, no crash-reporting services, no advertising networks, no social networks, no developer servers. Veto does not track you across apps or websites.

6. Notifications, widgets and Live Activities

Veto can show local notifications when a blocking window starts or ends, a home-screen widget showing the time left in your day, and a lock-screen activity showing that blocking is on. All of these are scheduled and rendered on your device. No push notifications are sent from servers, because there are no servers.

7. Deleting your data

You can delete individual rules from within the app at any time. Deleting the Veto app from your device removes all data Veto has ever stored, including rules, settings, statistics and the app-group container. Because nothing is stored anywhere else, deletion is complete and immediate.

8. Children

Veto is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone — child or adult — because it does not collect personal information at all. A parent or guardian may use Veto on their own device to restrict apps in line with Apple's Family Sharing and Screen Time features.

9. Third-party services

The app contains no third-party analytics, advertising or tracking SDKs. Veto is distributed through the Apple App Store; Apple's own privacy practices apply to the App Store itself.

10. Changes to this policy

If Veto's data practices ever change, this policy will be updated and the "Last updated" date above will change. Material changes will also be reflected in the app's App Store release notes.
